# Ascent scene art

The mountain backdrop, close ice wall and climber poses are original generated illustrations, not photographs or claims of topographic accuracy. They evoke Everest and the surrounding Himalaya. The route, camera, parallax, frame timing and day/night lighting remain editable SVG/CSS/JavaScript. All source art was generated with the built-in imagegen tool, not a fallback CLI.

- Generation mode: built-in imagegen tool, `stylized-concept` use case for the background and transparent assets for the wall/climber.
- Original: `assets/img/ascent-everest-illustration.png` (1086 × 1448).
- Optimized background sources: `assets/media/ascent-everest-illustration.webp` (1086 px), `ascent-everest-illustration-800.webp`, `ascent-everest-illustration-640.webp`, and `ascent-everest-illustration-640.avif`.
- The mobile AVIF source is deliberately smaller to keep the large scene from delaying first paint. The site serves responsive formats from `<picture>`; no external image service is used.
- Foreground source: `assets/img/ascent-icewall-illustration.png`; responsive alpha WebP: `assets/media/ascent-icewall-800.webp` and `ascent-icewall-480.webp`, with `ascent-icewall-480.avif` on supported mobile browsers.
- Earlier orange climber source: `assets/img/ascent-climber-frames.png`; its first transparent pose remains at `assets/media/ascent-climber-01.webp` for provenance, but is no longer loaded by the page. That sheet did not provide the current black-and-white frame sequence.
- Earlier black-and-white climber source: `assets/img/ascent-climber-alpine.png` (1086 × 1448, transparent); optimized single pose: `assets/media/ascent-climber-alpine.webp`. It informed the current clothing, gear and view angle. The hinged raster-limb rig that cut up this painting was removed after it repeatedly produced broken-looking joints.
- Current animation sources: four newly generated transparent 2 × 2 sheets, `assets/img/ascent-climber-clean-right.png`, `ascent-climber-clean-crossover-left.png`, `ascent-climber-clean-left.png`, and `ascent-climber-clean-crossover-right.png`. Every panel is an independently drawn **complete figure** with its own torso, arms, legs, boots, tools and gear. The former six pose sources were removed after the clean-sheet redraw; Git history retains them. `npm run build:climber-frames` selects and orders 16 complete poses, aligns each by its blue goggle lens, removes tiny disconnected crop artifacts, and exports `assets/media/ascent-climber-frame-01.webp` through `-16.webp` (400 × 533). `node scripts/preview_climber_frames.cjs` writes an inspection contact sheet to `test-results/`.
- October 12 right-profile correction: replaced the crossover-left, left-leading, and crossover-right sheets after browser screenshots revealed straight-back poses and a hidden leg. All 16 current frames retain the right-facing rear-three-quarter camera view established by the right-leading sheet. Both legs and crampon boots are visible in every frame, and the ice-axe pick ends face the wall on the right. The rendered asset/script URL is `ascent-12`. The pose sequence remains an illustration with discrete frames; a more physically faithful alternating-foot cycle would need dedicated character animation rather than further swapping independently generated stills.
- The site warms the remaining 15 WebPs after the first page paint or on the viewer's first scroll interaction, and shows exactly one complete pose at a time. Arms, legs, jacket and gear are never independently rotated or resized. A two-image crossfade was previously rejected because it made the climber translucent and duplicated tools/feet; an optical-flow intermediate produced broken extra legs. The scene's route/camera update continuously, while the artwork remains 16 discrete poses per entry rather than a filmed or physically simulated climb. Reduced-motion holds the first pose. The outfit is unbranded black-and-white alpine technical gear.
- The Home identity section reuses the existing Everest illustration through its 800 px WebP derivative. No new mountain asset was generated.

Movement references: [Petzl's ice-climbing technique video](https://www.petzl.com/int/en/sport/tech-tips/basic-techniques/ice-climbing-technique---the-basics/Cascade-gestuelle_SPO_a771r000000PFoJAAW.html), [Petzl's Ice Climbing Basics access book](https://s3.amazonaws.com/Petzl_home_Prod/Accessbook/ACCESSBOOK-ICE-CLIMBING-EN-2018.pdf), and [REI's ice-climbing guidance](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/ice-climbing-waterfalls-alpine-walls.html) emphasize stable tool/foot placements and rising through the legs. These sources informed the illustration's rhythm; the individual drawn poses are not climbing instruction or a physical simulation.
