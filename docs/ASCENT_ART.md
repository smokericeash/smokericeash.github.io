# Ascent scene art

The mountain backdrop, close ice wall and climber poses are original generated illustrations, not photographs or claims of topographic accuracy. They evoke Everest and the surrounding Himalaya. The route, camera, parallax, frame timing and day/night lighting remain editable SVG/CSS/JavaScript. All source art was generated with the built-in imagegen tool, not a fallback CLI.

- Generation mode: built-in imagegen tool, `stylized-concept` use case for the background and transparent assets for the wall/climber.
- Original: `assets/img/ascent-everest-illustration.png` (1086 × 1448).
- Optimized background sources: `assets/media/ascent-everest-illustration.webp` (1086 px), `ascent-everest-illustration-800.webp`, `ascent-everest-illustration-640.webp`, and `ascent-everest-illustration-640.avif`.
- The mobile AVIF source is deliberately smaller to keep the large scene from delaying first paint. The site serves responsive formats from `<picture>`; no external image service is used.
- Foreground source: `assets/img/ascent-icewall-illustration.png`; responsive alpha WebP: `assets/media/ascent-icewall-800.webp` and `ascent-icewall-480.webp`, with `ascent-icewall-480.avif` on supported mobile browsers.
- Earlier orange climber source: `assets/img/ascent-climber-frames.png`; its first transparent pose remains at `assets/media/ascent-climber-01.webp` for provenance, but is no longer loaded by the page. That sheet did not provide the current black-and-white frame sequence.
- Earlier black-and-white climber source: `assets/img/ascent-climber-alpine.png` (1086 × 1448, transparent); optimized single pose: `assets/media/ascent-climber-alpine.webp`. It informed the current clothing, gear and view angle. The hinged raster-limb rig that cut up this painting was removed after it repeatedly produced broken-looking joints.
- Current animation sources: four transparent 2 × 2 sheets in `assets/img/ascent-climb-step-left.png`, `ascent-climb-hand-to-left.png`, `ascent-climb-step-right.png`, and `ascent-climb-hand-to-right.png`, plus the two full-size intermediate poses `ascent-climb-left-foot-mid.png` and `ascent-climb-right-foot-mid.png`. These were generated with the earlier black-and-white climber as a visual reference, then visually curated into a 17-pose cycle: high right tool with rising left crampon; handover to the high left tool; rising right crampon; handover back to the right tool. The intermediate drawings close the largest leg-position jumps. `npm run build:climber-frames` extracts/optimizes them to `assets/media/ascent-climber-frame-01.webp` through `-17.webp` (400 × 533). The older three cycle sheets remain as provenance but are not used in this revision.
- October 12 pose correction: the five revised sources `step-left`, `hand-to-left`, `step-right`, `right-foot-mid`, and `hand-to-right` redraw the straight supporting leg almost vertically beneath the hip, replacing the extreme diagonal split visible in earlier frames. The bent stepping leg and alternating arm poses remain. The source sheets are canonical; regenerate WebPs with `npm run build:climber-frames`. The rendered image URL uses `?v=ascent-10` so returning visitors receive the revised art.
- The site warms the remaining 16 WebPs after the first page paint or on the viewer's first scroll interaction, and shows exactly one complete pose at a time. Each whole illustration is translated slightly so its blue goggles stay at a common anchor. Arms, legs, jacket and gear are never independently rotated or resized. We tried a two-image crossfade in the browser but rejected it because it made the climber translucent and duplicated tools/feet; we also rejected an optical-flow intermediate because it produced broken extra legs. The scene's route/camera update continuously, while the artwork remains 17 discrete poses per entry rather than a filmed or physically simulated climb. Reduced-motion holds the first pose. The outfit is unbranded black-and-white alpine technical gear.
- The Home identity section reuses the existing Everest illustration through its 800 px WebP derivative. No new mountain asset was generated.

Movement references: [Petzl's ice-climbing technique video](https://www.petzl.com/int/en/sport/tech-tips/basic-techniques/ice-climbing-technique---the-basics/Cascade-gestuelle_SPO_a771r000000PFoJAAW.html) and [Petzl's Ice Climbing Basics access book](https://s3.amazonaws.com/Petzl_home_Prod/Accessbook/ACCESSBOOK-ICE-CLIMBING-EN-2018.pdf) emphasize stable stance and rising through the legs. [Wyoming Mountain Guides' introductory course guide](https://wyomingmountainguides.com/ice-climbing/intro-to-ice-climbing-course-guide/) describes moving the opposite foot first from the high tool. These sources informed the illustration's rhythm; the website is not climbing instruction or a physical simulation.
